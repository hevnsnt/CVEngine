Approach

cvENGINE assesses hosts from the outside, the way an unauthenticated attacker on the internet would see them. It discovers hosts and subdomains, finds open ports and identifies the services and versions behind them, runs read-only template checks against web services, and reviews TLS certificates and mail records. Scans run from cvENGINE itself or from Sentinels, small scanning agents the client deploys; a Sentinel placed inside a network sees that network’s hosts the same outside-in way.

Each detected product and version is compared with the CVE advisories that name it. A version inside an advisory’s affected range is a version match: a strong lead, not proof, because vendors and Linux distributions often fix a flaw without changing the version number. Where a safe check exists, cvENGINE runs it to reproduce the issue on the host; a reproduced issue is confirmed, and the exact command is printed under its evidence so it can be repeated. An AI model helps triage findings and drafts the impact and remediation text; it never sets severity, CVSS or exploitation data, and candidates it classed as a likely false positive are left out of this report.

Severity ratings

Each finding gets one of five ratings. cvENGINE first takes the finding’s own severity: for a CVE, Critical when it is on CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) list or rated CVSS 9.0 or higher, High from CVSS 7.0, Medium from 4.0 and Low below; for a scanner or configuration finding, the severity its check states. It then caps that by what is known about this host (cvENGINE’s fire, smoke and ice heat), so the report agrees with the rest of cvENGINE:

RatingWhen it is used
CriticalKnown to be exploited or with a public exploit, proven on the host, and Critical on its own. A finding that rests on its version alone is never Critical unless the CVE is on the KEV list.
HighProven and High on its own, or known to be exploited (or with a public exploit) but not proven on the host yet.
MediumA warning sign rated Medium on its own, or a finding a check confirmed that is not known to be exploited but is High or Critical on its own.
LowNot known to be exploited and not proven, whatever its CVSS score; also low-severity scanner and configuration findings.
InformationalA CVE match whose fix the distribution backported, whose version could not be read, or with a CVSS below 4.0; and findings their check rates informational.

Configuration findings (certificates, TLS, mail records, cleartext services) are rated at most High. The rating of an issue found on several hosts is the highest of its hosts. {% if r.summary.heat_policy == 'legacy' %}This installation uses the legacy heat rule: fire is known to be exploited, a public exploit, CVSS 9.0 or higher, or likely to be exploited and CVSS 7.0 or higher; smoke is CVSS 7.0 or higher or a raised exploitation probability; ice is everything else.{% else %}Heat follows the KEV rule: fire is a CVE on the KEV list or with a public exploit whose version is proven on the host; smoke is the same with the version only inferred; ice is everything else, whatever its CVSS score.{% endif %}

Basis

  • Confirmed: a check on the host reproduced the issue.
  • Likely: the version was proven on the host (its own inventory, its distribution’s tracker, or the component itself), but the flaw was not reproduced.
  • Detected: a scanner template or a configuration check observed it directly; not independently reproduced.
  • Version match: the version the service reports is inside the affected range. This suggests the flaw is present but does not prove it.

Limitations

  • Version-based findings. A backported patch, a hidden banner or a false version can make a version match wrong in either direction. Treat it as a lead to confirm on the host.
  • Outside-in and unauthenticated. cvENGINE does not log in, cannot see software that does not answer on the network, and finds nothing on hosts that were out of scope, excluded, unreachable or not yet scanned. No finding is not the same as safe.
  • Point in time. Results are as of the scan dates above. A status of fixed is what a person recorded, until a later scan agrees.
  • Checks are read-only. Template checks and probes do not exploit, change or damage anything. A check that could not run leaves a finding as a version match.
  • Left out. Candidates the triage classed as a likely false positive or not applicable, CVE matches the AI ruled out, and informational detections are not listed{% if not r.include_closed %}, nor are findings marked fixed, accepted risk or false positive{% endif %}.

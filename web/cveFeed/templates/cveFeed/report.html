How to read the findings

  • Version-based findings. A CVE match marked “version-based” rests on the version a service reports being inside the range the advisory names. Vendors and Linux distributions often fix a flaw without changing the version number (a backported patch), and a banner can be hidden or false. Treat such a finding as a lead that needs confirmation on the host, not as proof. A finding marked “confirmed” was reproduced by a check recorded under its evidence, with the exact command a person can repeat.
  • Outside-in and unauthenticated. cvENGINE looks at hosts from the network the way an outsider would. It does not log in, cannot see software that does not answer on the network, and finds nothing on hosts that were out of scope, excluded, unreachable or not yet scanned. No finding is not the same as safe.
  • Point in time. Results are as of the scan dates above. Findings can appear after a fix or disappear after a change that was not a fix; a status of fixed is what a person recorded, until a later scan agrees.
  • Checks are read-only. Template checks and probes do not exploit, change or damage anything. A check that could not run, or that the scanner’s limits skipped, leaves a finding as version-based.
  • AI assistance. An AI model helps triage findings and write explanations. It never sets severity, CVSS or exploitation data, and its remediation text is guidance to be reviewed by the owner of the host.
  • Left out. Candidates the triage classed as a likely false positive or not applicable, CVE matches the AI ruled out, and informational detections are not listed{% if not r.include_closed %}, nor are findings marked fixed or accepted risk{% endif %}.

# Front end

The interface is server-rendered page shells plus plain ES modules that fill them from the REST API (`/api/v1`) and a few UI-only JSON endpoints under `/cve/api/`. The UI and agents share the same project-scoped code. There is no build step, no framework, and no CDN.

## Design

`web/static/cve/cve.css` is a light, monochrome design system: ink on neutral grays, wide-tracked caps for labels, and fine corner marks on the main panels. Color is rare and means something: red and amber for severity and threat tier, slate for low, a green dot for healthy, and brand orange for running state and the active navigation marker. Type is [Geist](https://github.com/vercel/geist-font), vendored.

- **Shell** (`base.html`, `shell.js`, `cveFeed/nav.py`): a left rail from 1024px wide with ten entries, in this order: **Overview**, **Findings**, **Hygiene**, **Hosts**, **Suppliers**, **Scans**, **Reporting**, **Scanners**, **MCP** and **Bug bounty** (the last only for people authorised for it; with no project selected the rail shows Overview, Graph, Reporting, Scanners and MCP, because Findings, Hygiene, Suppliers and Scans need a project; **Reporting** is always there, since its page picks the project or organization itself). **Which entry is highlighted:** exactly one entry per page, or none. The page's own entry wins: on the Hygiene page only **Hygiene** is current (not Findings), on the Suppliers page only **Suppliers** (not Hosts); every other page is current under the entry it belongs to (Secrets, Hosts, Targets and Graph under Hosts; Matches, Changes and the CVE catalog under Findings; Activity under Scans; Sentinels under Scanners). Utility pages (My account, Administration, Settings, API keys, About, the AI model setup, the welcome wizard) belong to the person, not to the work: no rail entry is current on them, the account control at the foot of the rail (the Account button on a phone) is, and so is the one account-menu item that opens the page. Each current item carries `aria-current="page"`. The rail never scrolls on a 1280x720 screen; the phone's bottom bar keeps four entries and Hygiene, Suppliers, Reporting, Scanners, MCP and Bug bounty are in the More sheet. **MCP** (`/cve/mcp/`, `cveFeed/mcp_page.py`) is how to connect an AI agent (the server address and a copyable setup for Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code and curl), what to ask it, and the **History** of every MCP tool call made as you (an App Admin sees everyone's): when, the key and agent, the tool, its arguments with secrets masked, and how it ended; results are never kept, heartbeats and polls that found no work are left out, and calls are kept `CVENGINE_MCP_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS` (90) days (`agentapi/mcp_log.py`). Settings, Administration and About sit in the footer, each only for the roles that can open them. Pages that used to have their own entry are tabs inside the one they belong to (Hygiene and Suppliers are also tabs, under the same names as their entries, so their sibling pages stay one click away): Overview (Overview, Situations), Findings (Findings, CVE matches, Hygiene, Changes, CVE catalog), Hosts (Hosts, Targets, Graph, Secrets, Suppliers), Scans (Scans, Activity) and Scanners (Scanners, Sentinels, This project). Every page has one name: the rail, its tab, the Ctrl+K palette, the menus and the page title all use it, and a plain sentence under the title says what the page is for. Below 1024px a top bar, a bottom tab bar (the first four entries) and a "More" sheet take over. The top bar holds the palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K or "/") and Add targets. The palette jumps to pages, projects, organizations, and actions (Add targets, Keyboard shortcuts, Select several rows, and **Run saved search: <name>** for each saved search), and from two characters on it runs a [host search](SEARCH.md) with filter suggestions (Tab completes), a live preview of up to six hosts, and a syntax help sheet. Enter runs the highlighted row; pick the **Search hosts for "<query>"** row to open the Hosts page with the query. When your projects span two or more organizations, an **organization switcher** in the top bar narrows the project list to one organization (or **All organizations** in the All projects view).
- **Dashboard** (the **Overview** page, called Live before 1.4): opens on **All projects** when you can see two or more, and on one project with `?project=<slug>`. Top to bottom (a phone stacks the columns):
  - **Head row.** The page tabs (Overview, Situations) and **Email this weekly**, which opens the Reporting page on its Email tab with the project (or the organization the All projects view is narrowed to) already picked. There is no period control: every "this week" is the last 7 days.
  - **Status.** A pill (**Needs you now**, **Watch**, **All clear**, or **Not scanned yet**), when the page last asked, and, for an App Admin only, one line about the live Sentinels and their scan slots ("2 Sentinels live, all 4 scan slots busy"). Under it the headline and a paragraph, **templates filled from counts, not written by a model** (`cveFeed/overview.py`): "1 host has a confirmed vulnerability, and 26 more likely have one and need review." (hosts with a proven finding, then hosts with likely findings; with nothing proven it leads with the likely ones; high-priority hygiene alone says so; "Nothing needs you right now." when nothing does; nothing finished scanning in 7 days is said in words), then the top threat of the week that matches a host by version ("Four new critical PHP flaws this week match 3 of your hosts by version.", dropped when none does) and how many hosts were exposed a week ago and now ("Externally facing hosts went from 26 to 55 since last Saturday.").
  - **Four tiles**, each a link to the page it counts, with a 7-day change (worse is red, better is green, with the arrow as well as the colour) and a 14-day sparkline: **Exposed hosts** (live, "of N hosts"), **Needs attention** (live, "hosts to look at": the hosts with a proven, likely or high-priority hygiene situation, the same number as the email report's **Needs attention** tile; it opens Situations filtered to **High hygiene or worse**), **Critical findings open** ("N high") and **Mean time to fix**. The last two, and the series and 7-day change of all four, come from the daily snapshots (`StatsSnapshot`), so they are as of the day in the tile's tooltip; the Needs attention change compares only with a day a week back where every project of the scope has the figure, as the email does, and shows none without one.
  - **This week's threats.** The digest the weekly email uses (CISA KEV additions, new critical CVEs with a public exploit or a high exploitation probability, and Cloudflare Zero Wire briefings when an App Admin has them on), with the CVEs of one product grouped on one card: badges (CVE count and top CVSS, KEV, exploitation probability, "Remote, no login" only when every CVE's CVSS vector says network and no privileges), a title built from the CVEs, the first sentence of each, **Fix: update PHP to ...** from the fixed versions, the CVE ids, and an impact panel ("3 hosts match by version", "+4 hosts run PHP, version unknown", split by project in the All projects view) with **See the N hosts** and a link to the briefing or the CVE. Each big number, and each project's share in the sentence under it ("1 MarinoMgmt", "all in OceanX"), is a link to the Hosts page listing exactly the hosts it counts, and **See the N hosts** opens the same list as the big number (the version-matched hosts when there are any): the server names them (`?names=a.example,b.example`, or `?names=acme/a.example,widgets/b.example` on the All projects page; a host of a project outside the summary is never named) with a `?label=` the Hosts page shows as a removable chip, and the page's count equals the card's: the card counts machines as the Hosts page folds them and the link names each machine once, under the name the Hosts page shows. More than 200 hosts, or a list too long for one request line, falls back to a broader search (the CVEs, or `tech:"PHP"`) and the chip says so. Threats that reach no host fold into one line ("6 more checked: none of your hosts run them"). Text from the feed is cleaned, cut and drawn with `textContent`.
  - **Live discovery.** The live asset graph (with a **Group CVEs** toggle), a **Scanning now** row of chips (click one to find it on the graph), host, IP and open-port counts, and a ticker of what discovery just saw. A badge beside the title shows the followed scan while it is queued ("queued, N ahead"), running or failed, and links to it; a finished scan shows no badge. **Watch it live** after Add targets follows the new scan. Unchanged by the redesign: the card moved, its markup and script did not.
  - **Fix first** (left) and, stacked on the right, **What changed** and **AI activity**, in columns of equal height. Fix first is the first 6 to 8 lines of the [Situations](SITUATIONS.md) ranking, with a rank, the host, a chip (**Proven**, **Likely real**, **Hygiene · high**), the project, one plain line and the first action as a button; hosts of one project that share a first action are one line ("3 OceanX hosts"), and **All N hosts that need you** opens Situations. What changed is the week's inventory changes: hosts newly exposed, pages showing personal data, certificates expired or due in 30 days (with the next one) and hosts found and retired. AI activity is the engine's current work, newest first, with repeats folded into a "x N" count; the activity box keeps its last 100 lines, older activity is on the Activity page.
  - **Health** (folded, one `<details>` each, the older panels moved here unchanged): **Hygiene** (with the three biggest), **Longest unfixed** (the oldest critical or high issue; only when there is one), **Internal networks** (only when a network is declared), **Riskiest hosts** (formerly Map; one tile per live host that scores over 40, highest score first, colored by threat: up to 400 are built and as many as fit are shown; hover for the score, the worst CVE or finding and the score reasons), **Engine counters** (an **Exposed hosts** tile with a meter, **CVE matches**, **Scanner findings**, **Scan coverage**, **Certs expiring 30d**, **Queued, none running**, and the 90-day activity strip, loaded once opened) and the **CVE feed** (Fire, Smoke and Ice buttons filter catalog advisories, not findings on your assets).
  - The status, tiles, threats, Fix first and What changed come from one summary, `GET /cve/api/overview` (same scope and permission rules as `/cve/api/stats`: `?project=<slug>` or `_all`, narrowed to the projects the caller can see), shared for a minute by everyone who sees the same projects and polled with the counters' pace; it never reads the AI activity log. A strip says how many changes happened since you last looked. With one project selected, **Download PDF report** sits in the head row ([PDF reports](#pdf-reports)).
  - The Changes page has a 7 and 30 day area chart, and the Scans page an 18-week scan heatmap.
- **Mobile**: 16px gutters, 44px touch targets, stacked-card tables, visible focus rings, and reduced-motion support.
- **Safe by construction**: DOM is built with `textContent` (the only `innerHTML` uses are the QR image on My account and fixed icons in the row menu and the Add targets dialog), and destructive actions use an in-page confirmation.

Every stylesheet and script URL carries a hash of the file's content (`?v=`, including the import map the ES modules use). nginx sends `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` for a URL with a well-formed `?v=` hash and `no-cache` for any other `/staticfiles/` URL (the fonts, which the stylesheet names), so a browser or CDN never asks again for a file it holds, and never serves one release's stylesheet with the next release's pages: a changed file has a new URL. Pages also inline a small critical style (`critical_style` in `cveFeed/templatetags/assets.py`, before the stylesheet link) and every inline icon has `width` and `height`, so a stylesheet that arrives late cannot show a giant glyph or a jumping layout.

## CVE catalog

`/cve/cves/` searches the CVE catalog, not your inventory (for your hosts, use the Hosts page; see [SEARCH.md](SEARCH.md)).

- **Query.** Something that looks like a CVE id (`CVE-2024-6387`, `2024-6387`, `cve-2024`, or a bare number of four or more digits) matches the id. Anything else matches the vendor, product and package names a CVE lists as affected, then its title, ignoring case: `ssh` finds OpenSSH, `php` finds PHP. Version ranges are never searched.
- **Order.** The exact id first, then ids that end with what you typed, then product hits, then title hits, newest first within each.
- **Filters.** Severity (CVSS band: critical 9+, high 7+, medium 4+, low) and KEV only. Fifty rows a page.
- **Rows.** Id, title, heat (fire, smoke, ice), severity, KEV, exploit, EPSS, published date, and how many of your hosts match. A match the AI reviewer ruled out is not counted.
- **Detail drawer.** Summary, CVSS and vector, EPSS, affected products with their ranges, references (http and https only), and your matching hosts, each linking to its host record and its match.
- **Address.** `?q=`, `severity`, `kev=1`, `page` and the open CVE (`id`) live in the address, so a link shows the same view. Ctrl+K offers **Search CVEs for "..."** for any bare word or id.
- **Scope.** The catalog is the same for everyone. Host counts and matching hosts come only from projects you can see (and `?project=` narrows the API to one).
- **API.** `GET /cve/api/cves?q=&severity=&kev=1&page=` returns `{count, page, page_size, has_next, items}`. `GET /cve/api/cves/<CVE id>` returns one CVE with `affected`, `references`, `host_count` and `hosts` (up to 200).
- **Speed.** Migration `0147` adds trigram indexes (`pg_trgm`) over the product names, the title and the id, so a substring search stays fast on a large catalog. Without the extension the search still works, only slower.
- **Code.** `cveFeed/cve_explorer.py` (query and ranking), `static/cve/explorer.js` (the page), `static/cve/explorer-logic.js` (address state, the palette row, formatters; tested by `tests/test_cve_explorer.mjs`).

## Confirm it yourself

**Confirm it yourself** is drawn for every kind of finding, from a guide the server builds by rules (no AI call) with the finding's own host, port and URL validated and filled in (`confirm` in `get_finding`): where to look, what to look for (the scanner's own evidence, shown as data), read-only steps to copy, how to read the result, and the fix. A CVE match's guide comes from `cveFeed/confirm_guide.py`, a TLS audit result's from `cveFeed/tls_catalogue.py`, and every other kind's from `cveFeed/finding_confirm.py`:

- **OpenVAS**: the test (NVT) OID with its Greenbone SecInfo page, the version OpenVAS claimed and how sure it was (its quality of detection, said plainly when it is only an inference: "OpenVAS inferred this version; it was not read from the service"), a remote hint where one means something (the `X-Powered-By` or `Server` header of a web runtime, the SSH greeting, else an `nmap -sV` of the port), the on-host checks (`php -v`, `php -m`, the installed package and its changelog lines for the CVEs: Red Hat backports fixes without raising the version), the distribution trackers of every CVE (Red Hat's when the banner names Red Hat), and notes when the banner does not name the product or the version is on a release line upstream no longer fixes.
- **nuclei**: the template id with a link to it, the matched URL, `nuclei -duc -ni -t <template> -u <url>` (labelled an active check: a template can send more than the request the scan stored), a `curl` that repeats the stored request when it was a plain GET or HEAD (credentials and cookies left out, and said so; any other method becomes no command), what the matcher looked for, and the request excerpt with secrets redacted.
- **WPScan**: the plugin, theme or core version the scan read and how (its `found_by`), the fixed version, a `curl` of the plugin's `readme.txt` (Stable tag), the theme's `style.css` (Version) or the core generator tag, and the read-only WP-CLI command.
- **Any other source**: where it was found and what the scanner reported, a GET of the page or an `nmap -sV` of the port, and the AI review's advice as plain steps marked as the model's words (never a command).
- **Hygiene** findings get one read-only check per family: `openssl s_client` (with `openssl x509`) for a certificate, `-tls1_1` and `nmap --script ssl-enum-ciphers` for a legacy protocol, `dig TXT`/`CAA` for SPF, DMARC, DKIM, CAA and MTA-STS, `curl -I` for HSTS, the greeting and `AUTH TLS` (`nc`, `openssl s_client -starttls ftp`) for cleartext FTP, `nmap -sV` for Telnet and the r-services. An open web route gets an anonymous GET.

A value that does not validate never reaches a command (the step is left out instead), and when no guide can be built the panel still shows where the finding is and what to compare. cvENGINE never runs these commands. Under the guide, the panel says what is true when no check has run (`validation.automatic` in `get_finding`: whether this kind of finding has an automatic check, its validation lane, and why the sweep will not queue it): a check or AI review that is queued or running and since when; "No automatic check exists for this finding; confirm it by hand with the steps above" for a scanner or hygiene finding (only CVE matches, unauthenticated databases and open routes have one); that triage has not started or finished; that the sweep (every 5 minutes) will queue it in its Fire, Smoke or Ice lane; or that it will not, and why (version unknown, reported by the host itself, ruled out).

## Polling and counts

Every poller on the live pages (the dashboard, the graph page and its SOC highlight, the Sentinels page, the scan notifications on every page) uses `static/cve/poll.js` instead of a bare `setInterval`: one request in flight at a time, the next round armed when the last finishes, every wait jittered by x0.85 to x1.15 so a room of tabs does not fire in step, no polling in a hidden tab (it starts again, at once, on `visibilitychange`; the scan notifications are the exception: they slow to a quarter of their pace instead of stopping, so a finished scan is still announced), the wait doubled (up to 16 times) after each failed request, and a `Retry-After` on a 429 or 503 honoured (capped at two minutes). The shared `get` remembers each URL's `ETag` and sends `If-None-Match`; a `304` resolves to `null`, which a poller reads as "nothing changed" and returns before parsing or redrawing (the stats, graph-live, discovery and activity endpoints, the Sentinels live overview and the scans list send one). The Sentinels page asks for a Sentinel's detail card only when its live row's `revision` (or `version_state`) moved, with a two-minute safety net. The Live graph and the graph page send the graph `?v=<version>` so an unchanged graph answers 304. A Scans list that is waiting on a running scan and the live counters stop while `document.hidden` too. On a list page the headline numbers (`/cve/api/stats`) are read every 8 seconds; the filter chip counts and a list's own count, which each cost a list query, are read when a headline number moved and otherwise every fourth tick. Findings reads its four type chips with one request. The coverage figure on Live and the trend strip are computed from what the API returns: when the Findings list is cut off (it is ordered by heat, not date) or the host search stopped counting at its cap, the page says so instead of presenting the numbers as exact.

## Retest on fix

A finding marked **Fixed** (a person in the detail pane, a row's menu, Select mode, the REST API, MCP, or an agent that reports a fix) is checked again: cvENGINE queues the same check that produced the finding's proof, and a Sentinel runs it through the validation dispatch (nothing runs on the server). A finding shows what that found, in plain words, in the Findings and CVE matches lists (the status chip, its tooltip names the Sentinel and the time), in the **Retest after the fix** card at the top of the detail, and in the status line under the **Remediation status** control:

- **Fixed, verifying**: the retest is queued or running.
- **Verified fixed** (with the date): the check ran, the host answered and it no longer reproduces the finding. Only this is green.
- **Still reproduces, reopened**: it does. The finding is open again (an activity line, an audit event, a note on the finding and a notification say so) and the new request and answer are its proof (the **Proven on this host** card).
- **Fixed, not verified: the reason**: it could not be told (no Sentinel could take the check, the host did not answer, the check failed, the check is no longer available). The finding stays fixed and is tried again with back-off, one day, then two, four and seven, never more than once a day, and for six tries at most. A host that could not be reached is never read as fixed.
- **Marked fixed, cannot be re-checked automatically**: no stored check or evidence to repeat (a finding with no validation hit, or one of a kind that has no validated check). It is never shown as verified.

**Retest now** (beside the status, for a finding marked fixed) runs the check again at once. A finding that is Fixed, then set to another status by hand, starts over. The **Status** filter of Findings also has **Verified fixed**, **Fixed, not verified** and **Reopened by retest**; their counts are in `retest` of the findings list (`cveFeed/counts.py`: `retest_q`, `retest_counts`), and a PDF report lists **Verified fixed** apart from **Marked fixed, not verified**. The AI reviewer never takes a finding up for review while it is marked fixed, and a finding a retest reopened is held as having proof. Code: `cveFeed/fix_retest.py`, `static/cve/retest-logic.js` (tested by `tests/test_retest_logic.mjs`, `tests/test_glance_proof.mjs`), `tests/test_fix_retest.py`. Re-verifying findings that are already verified fixed, on a schedule, is not done.

## Working in lists

- **KEV is shown whatever the heat.** A CVE match of a CVE listed as exploited carries a red **KEV** badge on the Findings and Matches rows and in the host's **Why** list (its tooltip says the match shows below fire only when its version is not proven), and both lists have a **Known exploited** filter (`?kev=1`, kept in the URL and saved views of Findings). Under the KEV heat policy a KEV match whose version is not proven is smoke (see [Fire, smoke, and ice](FIRE-SMOKE-ICE.md#from-the-feed-to-your-hosts)), so the badge and the reason are where it shows; fire means the version is proven on the host. Every CVE row (Findings, Matches, the host's **Why** list) shows the server's reason for its heat (`heat_reason`) as a short note and as the heat badge's tooltip: "on the KEV; version not proven", "not on the KEV and no known exploit", "on the KEV; version proven: template hit".
- **URL carries the view.** Hosts reads `?q=`, `sort=name`, `heat=fire|smoke|ice|clean|unscanned`, `internal=0|1`, `view=gallery`, and `host=<name>`. Defaults stay out of the link, so a bookmark is short.
- **CSV export** on Hosts and Findings downloads the current filter (UTF-8 with a BOM, 20,000 rows at most, 2,000 for Findings). A leading `=`, `+`, `-`, or `@` is escaped so spreadsheets do not run it.
- **Export all hosts** (Hosts page, in the page head beside the title; also in All projects) downloads every discovered host as a file to hand to a penetration testing company, made by the server with no row cap, not the list's CSV (that one exports the current filter with the internal columns). The button opens a small dialog: **Format** (Spreadsheet CSV, or a plain list for tools: one host name or address per line, unique and sorted, ready for `nmap -iL`), **Which hosts** (Internet-facing only, the default, or Include internal hosts) and **Include archived hosts** (off). In All projects the dialog also asks for an **Organization** (the ones you can see) and exports every project of it you can access. The file holds host names, IP addresses, open TCP and UDP ports, services, web URLs, project, organization, target and first and last seen, and no findings, notes, owners or scores; hosts the organization removed and excluded are never in it. A toast says how many hosts were exported, and each export is written to the audit log. See [Export all hosts](AGENT-API.md#export-all-hosts) for the file's columns and the REST route.
- **Row menu**: the `...` button on a row, or `.` on a focused row. Copy name or link, open in a new tab, pin, archive, or set a status.
- **Undo**: pin, archive, criticality, owner, and status changes offer Undo for 10 seconds when every row succeeded.
- **Keys**: `g` then `l` Overview, `a` graph, `h` Hosts, `c` Changes, `v` Activity, `m` matches, `f` Findings, `y` Hygiene, `t` Targets, `s` Scans, `r` Reporting, `b` Bug bounty (a letter works only when its page is reachable for your role; pages that share a rail entry are tabs inside it). In lists `j` and `k` move, Enter opens, `x` selects, `f` focuses the filter. On a host `1` to `7` switch tabs. On a host or finding `y` copies the name (a finding's link is `Y`), and on a CVE match `e` focuses the remediation status. `Esc` leaves Select mode. `?` shows the sheet. Keys are off while you type.

## PDF reports

**Download PDF report** is on the Overview page once a project is selected (not in All projects) and on a host's **Manage** tab; the **Reporting** page ([REPORTING.md](REPORTING.md)) makes the same report for a whole organization too, as a hosts report, and as a CSV file. It asks the server for a report, shows Queued and Building while it polls, and downloads the file when it is ready. A switch, **Include fixed and accepted-risk findings**, is off by default: a report lists open findings only. The report is built in a background task (`generate_report`, queue `cve_feed_queue`), stored under `/usr/src/scan_results/.reports/` with a random name, and offered only to the person who asked for 24 hours; each new report, and the weekly maintenance, remove whatever has expired. Any role that can read the project can export it, audit included. The same report is available to agents ([AGENT-API.md](AGENT-API.md#pdf-reports)).

A findings report reads like a penetration test report. In order:

1. **Cover**: the report title (*External Attack Surface Assessment*, or *Host Security Assessment* for one host), the client (organization or project), the report date, the assessment window (first to latest completed scan), the scope, who prepared it (the company name from Settings > Reports, or cvENGINE) and whether closed findings are included. Every page after it has a running header (title, client, date) and footer (prepared by, the Settings footer text or *Confidential*, Page N of M).
2. **Contents**, with page numbers.
3. **Executive summary**: a posture sentence, then plain sentences, built without an AI model, that name the most serious issues, how many hosts each affects and how sure we are (a confirmed issue is "confirmed by a check", a version match is "likely, based on the detected version only; not proven"); a table of issues, hosts and findings at each severity; up to five **key recommendations** (CVE issues of one product become one upgrade: "Upgrade PHP on 5 hosts to 8.1.29 or later"); and **positive observations** only where the data supports them (no KEV CVE matched, no expired certificate or cleartext login service when those checks ran, no critical issue).
4. **Summary of findings**: one numbered row per *issue* (#, finding, severity, basis, hosts, page), ordered by severity, then by hosts affected. An issue groups findings across hosts: CVE matches by CVE, scanner findings by template and title, hygiene findings by check ("Expired TLS certificate", 3 hosts).
5. **Detailed findings**: one write-up per critical, high or medium issue (at most 50): severity badge, why it has that rating, finding information, impact, evidence (the exact command that proved it when one ran), the affected hosts (host, project, detected version or URL, basis, status; 25, then "and N more"), remediation tracking (assignee, notes), recommendations (fixed releases and the remediation text), how to confirm a version match by hand, and references.
6. **Scope and methodology**: the targets, inventory and scan dates, how cvENGINE works (outside-in, Sentinels, version correlation versus proof, AI triage), the checks that ran, how severity is assigned and what each basis means, the limitations, and what the caps left out.
7. **Appendices**, lettered in order: low and informational issues, one line each; the asset inventory (200 hosts, then "plus N more in cvENGINE"; for one host, the host's services, banners and score); exposed secrets, only when there are any (masked values only); and the closed findings with who closed them, when and why, only when closed findings are included.

**Severity** has five levels, set in one place (`report_data.rate`). A finding starts from its own severity (a CVE: Critical when it is on CISA's KEV list or CVSS 9.0 or higher, High from 7.0, Medium from 4.0, Low below; a scanner or hygiene finding: its check's), then cvENGINE's heat caps it: fire keeps it, smoke is at most High, ice is Low (Informational for a backported fix, an unreadable version or CVSS below 4.0; Medium for an issue a check confirmed that is High or Critical on its own). A match that rests on its version alone is never Critical unless the CVE is on the KEV list, and a configuration finding is at most High. **Basis**: *Confirmed* (a check reproduced it), *Likely* (the version is proven on the host, the flaw not reproduced), *Detected* (a scanner template or configuration check observed it) or *Version match* (the reported version is in the affected range; not proof).

A hosts report has the cover, a summary (hosts on fire, smoke and ice, clean hosts, technologies and ports), the host table and the method page.

Matches the AI ruled out, and findings triage classed as a likely false positive or not applicable, are never listed (the summary says how many). The limits per report are in [CONFIGURATION.md](CONFIGURATION.md#pdf-reports); the counts always cover everything, and the PDF says what the limits left out. A large organization (about 1,700 findings) makes a report of 30 to 40 pages. Banners, titles, response text and finding text come from the hosts that were scanned, so they are escaped and printed as text, and the renderer runs in its own process with no network access and no file access beyond the page it was given.

## Internal hosts

A host inside a network its project declared internal (`projects/{slug}/internal-ranges`; scanned only by a Sentinel deployed there) is marked everywhere the API describes a host: **`internal`** (boolean) and **`network`** (the declared range, such as `10.20.0.0/24`, or null) on host rows (`hosts`, `search`, and the graph's `subdomain` nodes) and **`internal`** on findings (`vulnerabilities`, `findings`). `?internal=1` keeps only internal hosts, `?internal=0` only the others, on `hosts`, `search`, `vulnerabilities` and `findings`. The Hosts page shows a scope filter (All, External, Internal) once a visible project declared a network (`has_internal` in the search response), and both Hosts and Findings show a dashed **Internal** badge on the row; the Findings page has a Scope select.

`/cve/api/stats` carries an **`internal`** object: `has` (false when no visible project declared a network: leave the group out), `hosts`, `hosts_live`, `findings` (actionable scanner findings), `matches` (exposed CVE matches), `last_scan_at`, `sentinels_online`, and `networks`: one entry per declared range with `org`, `project`, `cidr`, `description`, `hosts`, `last_scan_at` (the last finished internal scan, or null), `last_error` (the error of the latest internal scan when it failed, else empty), `last_discovery_at`, `last_discovery_hosts` and `last_discovery_error` (the last live-host sweep of the range) and `sentinel: {state, online, total, last_seen}`, where `state` is `online` (an internal Sentinel is live), `offline` (some are registered, none live: the network is not being scanned, so a quiet network is not a clean one) or `none`. The headline totals in the same payload still include internal hosts; `internal` is the subset.

## Sentinels

`/cve/sentinels/` shows what every Sentinel you manage is doing, from `GET /api/v1/sentinels/live` (refreshed every 5 seconds). A summary bar counts the Sentinels that are checking in, the ones working now, and the tasks waiting for a Sentinel (work the planner queued is included), then one line saying what the work planner is doing (it opens into the routing note, the stages waiting and what is due by check). The page head is one line: the purpose sentence is cut at the end of the line, and **About this page** beside it opens the whole sentence (see **About this page** below). The summary's words wrap and never end in an ellipsis, and on a phone the summary is a sentence, not a strip that scrolls sideways. When one organization is all there is, its name does not head the list: the Sentinels start right under the summary, and its internal networks follow them. Each Sentinel is one row, so the Sentinels are the first thing on the screen: a small orb for its state, its name, what it is doing now (or its problem), its last check-in and the buttons it offers; what is wrong shows on the closed row without opening it: **Update failed** (a failed or stuck self-update), **Update available** (a newer version is out), **N failed in the last hour**, and the note the server keeps for it (why an update failed) in the row's sentence; the row opens into a card with the details below. The card has an animated orb for its state (Working, Idle and waiting for work, Offline, Waiting for approval, Suspended; a suspension's reason follows on the same line, `Suspended: <reason>`, and is not repeated under the card), an **Internal network** badge when it is internal (the server decides that: see [AGENT-API.md](AGENT-API.md#sentinels-scan-stage-agents)), its last check-in and version, the tasks it is running now and for how long, any problem it reported, counts (done and failed in the last hour, done in 24 hours, average task time), a bar for how busy its slots were in the last hour with the tasks it finished and its typical and worst queue wait, its recent tasks, and its tools, operating system and concurrency. Cards are grouped by the organizations each one covers (an instance-wide Sentinel sits under **Every organization**); a group folds, and the page remembers which in this browser. Each group also lists the internal networks its projects declared, with the host count, the last live-host discovery of the range (and its error, if any), and whether a Sentinel inside is checking in. **Suspend**, **Revoke**, and **Approve** or **Reinstate** (for a pending or suspended one) call the same `PATCH /api/v1/agents/{id}` the Agents admin surface uses. A Sentinel held back because another agent of its user is revoked also has **Delete <that agent>** (`DELETE /api/v1/agents/{id}`, after a confirm); the held one stays suspended until reinstated. The buttons show only on cards the viewer may decide on (`can_manage`: not their own unless App Admin, and not a user who also works for an organization they do not administer). The project's Sentinels tab shows `Suspended: <reason>` under a suspended row you may manage (other roles get no note), and **API keys, Agents in your organizations, Manage** has **Delete** for a revoked agent and says why an agent was suspended automatically. When a Sentinel reports its version and settings, its card also has **Update now** (it finishes the tasks it is running, then updates; in Manual update mode this is the only way) and **Settings** (name, where it runs, size, tasks at once from 1 to 10, and **What it may take**: Allow, Deny or Prefer and a cap per task kind, with who decided each, **Use default** to hand a kind back to the instance default, and no choice for a kind the Sentinel does not declare or its machine turned off; the most recent change wins whether it was made here or in the Sentinel's own `.env`, and each setting can be handed back to the Sentinel). A card shows a chip for each kind it has: allowed, denied (the tooltip says by whom, or that it is turned off on the machine) or withheld (the tooltip says until when). A kind turned off on every Sentinel that has it also shows in the strip at the top of every page, apart from an offline scanner.

The page also provisions Sentinels. Every dropdown starts on a disabled placeholder (nothing is pre-selected, the project included) and **Provision** stays off, with a line saying what is missing, until each has a real choice; a scope change clears the project or organization. A scope selector (**Project**, **Organization**, or **Instance (everything)**) with the matching field (a project picker, an organization slug, or nothing), **Where it runs** (external or internal) and **Size** (full, which also runs OpenVAS, or light) selectors, and **Provision** call `POST /api/v1/projects/{slug}/sentinels/enroll?format=blob` (needs the admin role in the project or its organization), `POST /api/v1/orgs/{slug}/sentinels/enroll?format=blob` (needs the admin role in that organization), or `POST /api/v1/sentinels/enroll?format=blob` (App Admin only), with `network` and `profile` parameters that choose the `.env` lines the setup uses. They show the one-time provisioning blob (valid 24 hours) to copy, with the matching install command (it downloads the client's `install.sh` and runs it, which creates an unprivileged `cvengine` user and installs in `/opt/cvengine-sentinel`; the blob goes in on standard input), the command that moves an older root-owned install to that user, and a short instruction to paste the blob into `CVENGINE_SENTINEL_BLOB` in the Sentinel client's `.env` for a Sentinel that is already installed. The blob is exactly the matching `sentinels/enroll` endpoint's own JSON payload, base64-encoded (`agentapi.sentinel_broker.encode_enrollment_blob`; no new crypto, no custom framing): the page does not build or serve a download itself, since the Sentinel client is a Docker Compose service that lives in its own repository, [hevnsnt/cvengine-sentinel](https://github.com/hevnsnt/cvengine-sentinel), not a per-OS package the web app ships. The `generate_sentinel_enrollment` MCP tool takes optional `project`/`org` arguments (at most one; neither means instance-wide) and returns the same blob plus the clone URL and the exact `docker compose` setup steps, so an agent with only Docker and shell access to a target machine (no prior context on Sentinels) can provision one unattended. See [AGENT-API.md](AGENT-API.md#sentinels-scan-stage-agents) and the [Sentinels guide](SENTINELS.md).

**Per project.** Once any Sentinel whose scope covers a project (named directly, via its organization, or instance-wide; pending, active or suspended, not revoked) has registered, that project gains a **This project** tab under Scanners to `/cve/{slug}/sentinels/`. The page lists the Sentinels covering the project from `GET /api/v1/projects/{slug}/sentinels` (status, live or offline, last heartbeat, the level it covers the project at, capabilities, and this project's task counts; another tenant's scope and the owner are not shown), with **Approve**/**Suspend**/**Revoke** only on the rows the caller may administer (`manageable`), plus this project's recent work from `GET /api/v1/projects/{slug}/sentinel-tasks`. Provisioning stays on the global page. A project administrator also sees an **Internal networks** card there: list, declare (CIDR plus description) and remove the private networks the project owns, via `projects/{slug}/internal-ranges`; the card stays hidden for anyone else (the API answers 403).

## Settings and extending

Under **Settings** (App Admins) there is one tab for each of these:

- **AI model**: the provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Ollama, vLLM, or LM Studio) in a **Connection** block (API key, address under Advanced for a hosted provider, one status line and **Test connection**), a table **Which model does which job** (three drop-downs filled from the provider's live list: Everything else, Finding review, Operations check; the saved model is always an option, **Other** takes a typed name), one **Save AI settings** with an unsaved-changes note and what is in force after a save, **Send a test message**, the daily budgets for validation planning and for CVE guidance written by the model, the **CVE catalog seed** card, and the **Auto-fire** card. The provider list is grouped (**Hosted**: Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter; **Your own server**: Ollama, LM Studio, and "vLLM, llama.cpp or another OpenAI-compatible server", which is also where LiteLLM and gateways go). The **Request settings** section (collapsed until something in it is set) holds the request timeout, connect timeout, reply limit, context window (Ollama only), JSON mode, reasoning and keep-alive (Ollama only). Each box says in the line under it what is in force and why: set here, from a `CVENGINE_AI_*` variable in the server's environment (a value here replaces it), or the provider's default (an empty box). A value out of range is flagged under its box and nothing is saved until it is fixed; a short reply limit, reasoning on a limit under 4,096 tokens, or a long reply (a limit of 16,384 tokens or more, or high reasoning) on a request timeout of 300 seconds or less shows a warning (requests are not streamed: one that times out here is still billed by the provider). Only the boxes the chosen provider uses are checked, so a leftover mistake in a hidden one (the Ollama-only context window) cannot block a save. **Send a test message** keeps its result on the page: "Connection OK", or the failure with what to change, and the line above the buttons says when the connection was last verified. The settings are saved by `PUT /cve/api/settings/ai` and described in [Configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#ai-requests).
- **API keys**: the one place for OSINT and scanner keys. Choose **Organizations** (a key applies to all their projects, present and future) or **Projects** (a key overrides the organization's), then add, change or remove a key per service. Secrets are write-only. The Chaos key also has an instance-wide fallback. Organization admins set organization keys from Administration, Organizations.
- **Tailscale**: connect a tailnet to a project ([TAILSCALE.md](TAILSCALE.md)).
- **OpenVAS**: switch the optional add-on on or off and choose which organizations and projects it scans ([OPENVAS.md](OPENVAS.md)). Organization admins choose what their own organizations scan from Administration, OpenVAS.
- **Notifications**: Slack, Discord, Lark and Telegram.
- **Email**: the SMTP server cvENGINE sends through. A person added with an email address gets an onboarding email with their username and a one-time link to choose a password ([Tenancy](TENANCY.md#onboarding-email)).
- **Scan policy**: the **Nuclei templates** switches (the whole installed library by default, or only the read-only templates; and **Allow checks that send credentials**, off by default: default-login and brute-force templates never run unless it is on). There is no Scan engines editor: every scan runs the same fixed pipeline, and the per-scan engine configurations inherited from reNgine were removed because nothing read them. An old `#engines` link opens this tab.
- **IP allowlist**, **Proxy** (the proxies the built-in scanner's traffic leaves through; private and tailnet addresses and the raw-socket tools stay direct, and your own Sentinels are not sent the list, see [Pipeline](PIPELINE.md#keeping-scans-fast-and-polite)), and **Reports** (company name, accent colors, executive summary text and footer for [PDF reports](#pdf-reports)).
- **Resources**: memory use and the job budget of each worker container.
- **Updates**: sets how updates are installed (Manual or Automatic), the update source (repository, branch, token), and shows a six-step timeline after you Approve (approved, the host picks it up, scans pause and drain, files update, services rebuild, scanning resumes), usually 3 to 6 minutes. When no host updater reports, it shows the one command to run, with a Copy button. For App Admins a slim bar on every page announces a new version (Review and update, Dismiss) and, once approved, shows the progress and greys the page while services rebuild ([UPDATES.md](UPDATES.md)).
- **Factory reset**.

The tabs that hold settings are described in [CONFIGURATION.md](CONFIGURATION.md#settings-in-the-app).

The Targets page's **Discover** button has a choice of mode next to it, the target's own by default; another mode runs the discovery in it and is kept on the target, so the work conductor rescans in it from then on, and the Discovery column shows the mode the server stored. A manual only target's choice starts on "Choose a mode" and Discover stays disabled until one is picked, so one click never turns it into a scanned target. The **Add targets** dialog takes an organization and project, `#Project` and `##Organization` grouped pastes, a file (`.txt`, `.csv`, `.list`), a live count, a discovery mode (Standard, Deep, Passive, or Just add), which is kept on each target and used for its later automatic scans (Just add means manual only: nothing scans it until a person picks a mode), a **Scope** for the new domain targets (Auto, the default and what every target was added with before; This host only; Include the parent domain), with the hint that a line written `=example.com` is added as exactly that host whatever the Scope says ([Exact-host targets](PIPELINE.md#exact-host-targets); the parsing and counting live in `addtargets-logic.js`, tested by `tests/test_addtargets_logic.mjs`), and an authorization tick (sent as `authorized: true` with the request; the server does not record it yet), then links to **Watch it live**.

`/cve/settings/` is backed by `cveFeed/settings_api.py` (`/cve/api/settings/...`), needs an App Admin session and CSRF, and is not part of the agent API. Secrets are write-only: responses say whether one is set, and a blank value keeps the stored one.

Page logic lives in `web/static/cve/app.js`. To add a section, add an entry to `SECTIONS` in `cveFeed/views.py`, a URL pattern, and a function in `pages`. The pages inherited from reNgine are retired and forward to their cvENGINE equivalents. The scan report (`/scan/create_report/<id>`) and the plain-text exports (`/scan/export/{subdomains,urls,endpoints}/<id>`) remain.

## How cvENGINE works

`/cve/how-it-works/` is a short explainer for people new to the tool, linked from the CVE feed card on the dashboard. It walks through five steps (discover everything you own, map every exposed service, match against the world's CVEs only when it fits, prove it safely, fix the right thing first) and a list of reasons to trust a finding. It carries a "Reviewed for version" stamp, `HOW_REVIEWED_VERSION` in `cveFeed/views.py`, which `tests/test_how_it_works.py` forces to match `web/.version` so the page is reread at every release.

## The rebuilding page

While an update is applied or the site stops answering, visitors see a "We are rebuilding cvENGINE." scene (two engineers in hard hats hold up a blueprint while a crane lifts the next piece into place, and trade a random line of banter every two seconds, one exchange at a time, with a "Usually 2 to 5 min" note) instead of an error. There are two paths to the same scene (`static/cve/maintenance.html`, one self-contained file that needs nothing from the application):

- **nginx** answers every path with it (status 503, `Retry-After: 120`) while the update flag file exists, and for any 502, 503 or 504 from the application. Served alone, it asks for its own address every few seconds and reloads the moment the real site answers.
- **Every page** loads the scene while the site is up (`static/cve/sitewatch.js`), asks `GET /cve/api/alive` every 15 seconds, and after two misses in a row covers itself with the scene (inert page, status line "The site is not answering. This page comes back by itself."). After two answers in a row it reloads. An App Admin's page that is watching an approved update drives the same cover itself with the update's phases (`updatebar.js`). Every page also carries the build that rendered it (`<meta name="cvengine-build">`, a fingerprint of the static `cve/` files and the page templates, computed when the web process starts and kept; it does not read `web/.commit`) and `GET /cve/api/alive` answers a signed-in caller with the build the server runs: when they differ (the page stayed open through an update, in a background tab, or behind back/forward) the page hard-reloads itself once per build per tab, or shows a "A new version is installed. Reload" bar when something was typed or a dialog is open. A page in the background asks when it comes back to the front. The Settings Updates section does the same check from the status it reads. Django's HTML pages are sent `Cache-Control: private, no-cache` so the browser always asks before showing one.

See also: [AGENT-API.md](AGENT-API.md), [TENANCY.md](TENANCY.md), [documentation index](README.md).

## Themes, preferences and the keyboard

Light is the default and is always on. Dark applies only when the viewer picks Dark, or Auto while the system is dark (account
menu, Theme). `static/cve/theme.js` runs in the head before the stylesheet and sets `data-theme` on `<html>`, so there is no flash.
Every color in `cve.css` is a token: the dark palette is one block of overrides after `:root`. `--ink` is the high-contrast
foreground and also the fill of selected rows and primary buttons, so in dark it becomes light and `--on-ink` (the text on that
fill) becomes dark. Text on solid fire, smoke, ice and ok fills stays white through `--on-solid`. Do not add a raw hex or `rgba()`
outside the token blocks; the one allowed exception is the QR code, which must stay black on white.

Per-browser preferences (localStorage, nothing sent to the server): theme (`cve.theme`), time zone (`cve.tz`, local or UTC),
saved views on Hosts and Findings (`cve.views.v1.<project>.<page>`, 12 per page), the last project used in each organization (`cve.org.last.v1`), Live graph pin positions (`cve.pins.<project|all>`), the "new since you last looked" baseline (`cve.seen.v1.<project>.<page>`), which Sentinels groups are folded (`cve.sentinels.folded`), the scope last chosen on the API keys page (`cve.keys.scope`) and
desktop notifications (`cve.notify.desktop`).

Modules: `views.js` (filters in the URL and saved views), `saved-searches.js` (saved searches with alerts, kept on the server), `bulk.js` (select mode and the action bar; actions run the single-item
request four at a time, although `hosts/bulk` exists), `keys.js` (vim-style keys and the `?` sheet), `seen.js` (new markers), `notify.js` (scan notices),
`prefs.js` (time zone formatting, theme chooser), `charts.js` (area chart, heatmap, sparkline; days are calendar days in the zone the page displays), `csv.js` (CSV export of the current filter, formula-safe), `rowmenu.js` (row actions menu), `palette-search.js` (Shodan-style filters, suggestions and host preview in Ctrl+K), `finding-glance.js` (the three cards at the top of a finding), `suppliers.js`, `inventory.js` (host inventory), `authcheck.js` (target sign-in), `report-export.js` (PDF report button, and `runReport` for the Reporting page), `reports.js`, `report-email.js` and `reports-logic.js` (the Reporting page and its Email reports tab; the logic is tested by `tests/test_reports_logic.mjs`, the page by `tests/test_reports_page.mjs`), `sentinel-live.js` and `sentinel-settings.js` (Sentinels page), `api-keys-panel.js` and `openvas-panel.js` (shared by Settings and Administration), `sitewatch.js` and `updatebar.js` (rebuilding page and update bar).
